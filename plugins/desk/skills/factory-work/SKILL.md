# Factory work

The factory is a Lean system and a terrarium: sealed and self-sustaining. Nothing in it needs a human hand, it keeps itself correct, its health is visible from outside, and zero never stands in for "no data".

Lean is the starting point for every question about the factory, not a lens applied when the operator asks. Before you propose, design or judge anything about it (a metric, a definition, a workflow, a priority), name the Lean concept that governs the question and say what it implies: value as the customer defines it, the eight wastes, flow and pull, jidoka and andon, poka-yoke, first-pass yield, standard work, go and see, or plan-do-check-act. Only then bring in what peers do, a proxy or engineering habit.

- **Designs and plans.** Every factory design and plan carries a "Lean reading" section that names the governing concept.
- **Reviews.** Put these questions in every reviewer brief, re-reviews included. Does this add waste? Is the error made impossible, or only discouraged? Does anything need a hand? Can a wrong or missing value read as a right one? Does a loop stop at noticing or filing? Every factory review brief also asks whether the change breaks a Lean principle or the terrarium.
- **Loops.** A loop is detect, fix the cause, verify, standardize, close. One that stops at noticing or filing is not a loop.
