The factory build now writes the data the store's Lean walk needs, without changing any existing figure. In the new data, a job's lead time splits into working time and idle time, and "waiting" means idle time and nothing else. Working time is the job's recorded turns, tool calls and subagents, less the time the evaluator labeled `waiting` in the same session. Idle time is the rest of the lead time, and each idle moment has one `waited_on`: `next_prompt` (the agent had stopped and the next prompt had not come, nights included), `api_retry`, `tool_failure`, `long_tool_call` (a successful call of five minutes or more), `queue_before_start` (no session of the job had started), `no_session` (no session was running) or `unknown` (no label or evidence says). Labeled classes and wastes describe working time only. A job with no labels yet counts all of its recorded work as working.

Each `jobs/<job>.json` timeline adds the job's workers, human turns and pull requests on the job clock, the lead window, and the work bursts and gaps. A pull request's time is listed only when the facts carry it, so a public desk's stay withheld. A burst is a run of working time split by 15 idle minutes or an operator turn, and `bursts_state` says how complete the intervals behind the bursts are. Bursts and gaps add up to the lead time, and each gap says what it waited on. Each burst's idle time inside it, plus the gaps, is the job's idle time. A burst's operator turns, pull requests and labeled time are envelopes, never a zero for no data. Each placed session also gets a swimlane file, `jobs/<job>/<session id>.json`, with the job's lane intervals and the evaluator's stretches, whose evidence points into that interval list. A swimlane file that would pass 4 MiB has its uncited intervals binned until it fits (`intervals_binned`, `bin_resolution_ms`). One still too large once every run is merged, because the intervals its stretches cite do not fit, says `over_budget: true` with the reason `over_budget_after_binning`.

Three new rollups sit next to the existing ones. Every figure in them carries its number state, and figures read from intervals take the formulas' completeness:
- `rollups/stackup.json` splits each job's lead time into `working` (by label, plus agent work and unlabeled work) and `idle` (by `waited_on`). The two add up to the lead time exactly. Each row names the job's Desk version. `basis: "wall_clock_in_lead_window"` says its totals do not match the muda rollup's sums.
- `rollups/tasks.json` gives each job's compact answer: lead, working and idle time, the value-adding part of working time, flow efficiency, idle time by `waited_on`, top three causes, longest gap and burst count. Its `flow_efficiency` is working time over lead time, stated as both are, so it agrees with the working and idle figures. `active_share_recorded` keeps the formulas' ratio, recorded active time inside the card's lead window over the lead time, which has no value when the card's dates are shorter than the work. In that case a closed job's `flow_efficiency` says `bound: "upper"` and its `idle_ms` says `bound: "lower"`, because the floored lead time is itself a lower bound; an open job's has no bound. The job files' `formulas.flow_efficiency` is unchanged.
- `rollups/causes.json` ranks causes across finished, fully labeled jobs with readable intervals: idle time by `waited_on`, and the labeled wastes of working time. It says `basis: "job_hours"`: a moment two jobs share counts once for each job.

Each `waiting` stretch now has a `waited_on`. A stretch where another worker of the job was working is not waiting: the new figures split that part off as unlabeled agent work. A worker's own turns and tool calls count as working. A parent's turn around the subagent it waits on does not, and neither does a `subagent` interval. The muda measures and the waste Pareto keep their meaning, and each Pareto adds `waiting_corrected_ms` and `agents_working_unlabeled_ms`, which add up to its `waiting` row. On the store's data when this was built, the correction moved 4.16 of 43.07 labeled waiting hours (14,985,367 of 155,036,895 ms, 9.7%) to agent work. The split looks at one session at a time, so a worker of the same job in a concurrent session does not yet split a wait. Labels from a session several jobs share make the new figures partial with `labels_from_shared_session`; that applies when the evaluator labeled the whole session or two jobs carry the same labels.

The [factory evaluator](skills/factory-evaluator/SKILL.md) now says that a stretch where any worker of the job is doing work is not `waiting`, and that the work should be labeled instead. Because that changes what a `waiting` label means, the rubric moves to version 3. New labels carry `"rubric":"3"`, and labels written under rubric 2 are still read and published as before.
